Social Media Claims

Designed to Keep Them Hooked

Social media addiction refers to the compulsive use of platforms like Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, or Snapchat despite negative consequences.

Tech companies rely on advertising revenue, and that means keeping users on their platforms as long as possible. To achieve this, they implement behavioral psychology models to manipulate user behavior. Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, and TikTok have faced allegations that their platforms are intentionally designed to be addictive, especially for children and teenagers.

If someone you love was affected by social media, you may qualify to file a lawsuit against the mega-corporations that own the platforms. Excessive use of these services may be linked to mental health problems such as anxiety, depression, eating disorders, and suicidal ideation. Help is available for families in need — find out if you qualify.

Life-Changing Effects

Life-Changing Effects Caused By Addiction

Social media addiction can affect nearly every part of a young person's life:

Mental Illness

ADHD/ADD, anxiety, depression, irritability, and psychological distress.

Physical Injuries

Eye strain, repetitive strain injuries, and sedentary lifestyle diseases.

Quality of Life

Escapism, poor school performance, sleep deprivation, and social isolation.

Withdrawal Symptoms

Aggression and disengagement from daily activities.

Eating Disorders & Body Image

Eating disorders, body dysmorphia, and self-image issues linked to constant comparison on social media.

Bullying & Cyberbullying

Bullying and cyberbullying, self-harm, and — in the worst cases — suicidal ideation and death by suicide.

Do You Qualify?

You May Qualify If…

You may qualify to file a social media addiction lawsuit if you or your child:

  • Developed an addiction to social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, or TikTok.
  • Experienced physical or emotional harm linked to that addiction.
  • Were under 18 years old at the time the addiction began.

Who is the claim for?

The form starts by asking who the claim is for: yourself, a loved one (age 13 to 17, 18 to 25, or 26 or older), or a loved one who has passed away. It then asks about the person's age, which apps they used (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Snapchat, or YouTube), whether they used them between the ages of 5 and 17, how many hours a day they spent on social media, what harm they experienced, what treatment they received, and whether another law firm already represents them.

Does my child have to testify?

No — a parent filing for a child does not need the child's testimony. Costs and fees are covered by legal representation, and there are no fees unless you win your case.

Can I file for a loved one who has passed away?

Yes. The claim form includes an option for a loved one who has passed away, and families in that situation can still seek justice. The experienced attorneys at Weitz & Luxenberg are at the forefront of the fight against big tech companies.

How long do I have to act?

The deadline to take legal action on social media-related claims is limited and varies by state. Waiting too long could cost you your right to seek justice, so acting immediately is critical to protect your rights. Financial recovery from a social media lawsuit can give families the support they need to help their child heal and move toward lasting stability and a better future.
